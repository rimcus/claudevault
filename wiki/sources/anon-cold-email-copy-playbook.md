# Cold Email Copy Playbook

**Author:** Unknown — practitioner playbook exported from Notion. References "2025 data" and claims 90 calls booked in the first 20 days of December with this system.
**Scope:** 9 chapters — Psychology, Structure, Subject Lines, Openers, CTAs, Copy Templates, Segmentation, Follow-Ups, Rules.
**Companion to:** [[sources/anon-cold-email-systems-guide]] — that guide covered infrastructure and pricing; this one covers pure copy mechanics.

---

## Core Thesis

Cold email copy fails at the message level, not just the infrastructure level. The reader decides whether to engage **in under 3 seconds**, asking only two questions: *"Is this relevant to me?"* and *"What's in it for me?"* Every copy decision should be optimized for those two questions — before it optimizes for anything else.

## Chapter 1: Psychology — The 5 Triggers

| Trigger | Why It Works | Example |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Recognition** | "They actually know my situation" | Referencing their specific tech stack |
| **Curiosity** | "I need to know more" | Teasing a result without explaining how |
| **Reciprocity** | "They gave me something first" | Sending value before asking for time |
| **Social Proof** | "Others like me got results" | Mentioning a similar company's outcome |
| **Low Friction** | "This is easy to respond to" | Yes/no questions, no calendar asks |

## Chapter 2: The Structure — 4-Element Recipe

The structure behind "90 calls in the first 20 days of December":

| Element | Purpose | Word Count |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| **Opener** | Prove relevance in first 5 words | 5–15 words |
| **Pain/Context** | Show you understand their world | 15–25 words |
| **Value** | What you can do for them | 10–20 words |
| **CTA** | Make it stupid easy to respond | 5–15 words |

**Total target: under 80 words.**

**Example:**
> Hey {{firstName}},
>
> {{company}} just fired a few sales reps, no? Usually when you cut the team, reply rates tank because the response speed and quality suffers.
>
> Built an AI agent trained on {{company}}'s info — it gets back to your leads within 60 seconds and books the call. Helped {{competitor name}} go from 1% to 5% replies last month.
>
> Want to check it out?

## Chapter 3: Subject Lines — 2025 Data

| Type | Avg Open Rate |
|------|-------------|
| Question + their company name | 38% |
| Lowercase, casual | 34% |
| 2–5 words | 32% |
| Title Case, formal | 24% |
| 7+ words | 19% |

⚠️ **Never track open rates in 2025/2026** — pixel tracking kills deliverability.

**Templates by style:**

| Style | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Curiosity hook | "{{firstName}}, noticed something on your Substack" |
| Role + outcome | "{{firstName}} - scaling outbound?" |
| Recent event | "re: the {{company}} expansion" |
| Pattern interrupt | "crazy idea" |
| Triggering hook | "deleting this?" |

**What to avoid:**
- ALL CAPS or excessive punctuation
- "Partnership opportunity" (screams sales email)
- False urgency ("URGENT: Read immediately")
- Emojis in subject lines (screams spam)
- Your company name (they don't know you yet)

## Chapter 4: Openers — The "Them First" Rule

> "Mention your lead's company before your own. It's always about them, never about you. If you talk about yourself first, you've already lost."

**5 Opener Types:**

| Type | Template | When to Use |
|------|---------|------------|
| Trigger Event | "Saw {{company}} just [event]. Congrats!" | Funding, launch, hiring |
| Observation | "Noticed your team is [observation]" | Tech stack, strategy |
| Content Reference | "Your post on [topic] hit home" | When they're active on LinkedIn |
| Mutual Connection | "{{mutual}} mentioned you're working on [X]" | Warm intro path |
| Pattern Interrupt | "Not sure if this is relevant, but..." | When direct feels too aggressive |

**Good vs. Bad:**

✅ "Noticed you're using HubSpot but no enrichment tool — curious if that's intentional"
✅ "Your CEO's post on the Series A challenges was spot on, regarding that…"
✅ "Saw you just promoted 2 SDRs to AEs — scaling fast"

❌ "Hope you're doing well"
❌ "My name is X and I help Y"
❌ "I came across your profile and thought we should connect"

## Chapter 5: CTAs — The Friction Test

> "Asking for a 30-minute call in your first message is like proposing marriage on the first date."

**Rule: one CTA per email.** Multiple options create decision fatigue.

**Friction hierarchy (ranked by reply rate):**

| CTA Type | Friction | Reply Rate |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| Yes/No question ("Interested?") | 🟢 Low | Highest |
| "Reply with 'yes' and I'll send you [X]" | 🟢 Low | High |
| "Mind if I send X?" | 🟡 Medium | Medium |
| "Can I share a quick video?" | 🟡 Medium | Medium |
| "15 mins this week?" | 🔴 High | Low |
| "Book a call here" | 🔴 High | Lowest |

**High-converting CTA examples:**
- "Interested?"
- "Want me to send it?"
- "Make sense to share more?"
- "Cool if I share how they did it?"
- "Would it be crazy to…"
- "Would you hate me if I sent you the [free stuff]?"

## Chapter 6: Copy Templates — 9 Structures

**Template 1 — The Honest Reach-Out:**
> "Hey {{firstName}}, honestly wasn't sure if I should reach out. Noticed {{company}} is [specific observation] — and I've been helping [similar companies] solve [specific pain]. Put together a quick breakdown of what's working for them. Want me to send it over?"

**Template 2 — The Content Reference:**
> "Hi {{first_name}} — saw your LinkedIn post on {{topic}} (the part about {{specific_point}} resonated). Regarding that, helped another company in a similar spot. If helpful, I can share a playbook we used to help {{similar_company}} improve {{metric}} by {{number}}. Interested?"

**Template 3 — The Trigger Event:**
> "Hi {{first_name}} — congrats on {{event}} (saw it in {{source}}). When teams scale {{thing}}, I usually see {{common_challenge}} show up fast. We helped {{similar_company}} handle that and got {{result_number}} in {{timeframe}}. Open to a quick idea on how to do the same at {{company}}?"

**Template 4 — The Hypothesis:**
> "Hi {{first_name}} — I might be off, but it looks like {{company}} has been {{specific_observation}}. Does that mean you're dealing with {{likely_problem}} — or no? Reason I'm asking: we fixed this for {{similar_company}} (they went from {{before}} to {{after}}). If I'm right, want the 3-step summary?"

**Template 5 — The Case Study:**
> "Hi {{first_name}} — quick one. {{similar_company}} was struggling with {{pain_point}} and got {{result_number}} after changing {{one_change}}. If you want, I can send the short case study (2 pages) — it's the exact playbook, not theory. Should I send it?"

**Template 6 — The Partnership/Integration:**
> "Hi {{first_name}} — noticed your team shipped {{recent_release}}. Our customers run into {{pain_point}} right after that stage, and a few have mentioned {{their_company}} alongside us. At {{your_company}}, we built {{what_you_do}} — paired with your {{their_capability}}, it could remove {{friction}} for shared users. Can I send you a quick Loom on this?"

**Template 7 — The Problem-Solution:**
> "Hi {{first_name}}, Noticed {{trigger}}. You're likely {{current_process}}. But it's a pain to {{pain}}. Normally it's down to {{root_cause}}. Could I share {{resource}} that gets {{dream_outcome}} for {{similar_company_type}}? Worth sending?"

**Template 8 — The Insight + Peer Proof:**
> "Hi {{first_name}}, Just saw {{relevant_article_or_post}} and {{specific_insight}} stood out. Wondering if your team's also facing {{specific_challenge}} because of that. Other {{industry_peers}} like {{brand_a}} and {{brand_b}} use {{your_product_or_method}} to {{use_case}} — which led to {{measurable_result}}. Mind if I send you a 90 sec vid on this?"

**Template 9 — The Ironic Hook:**
> "Hi {{first_name}} — it's always fun emailing the {{job_title}} of a company that {{ironic_relevant_thing}}. I'm guessing you care about {{main_goal}}. Problem is, {{existing_tools}} don't really let you {{desired_ability}}. With {{your_solution}}, teams can {{benefit}}. Mind if I send you the breakdown?"

## Chapter 7: Segmentation Playbook

**4 segmentation filters (must apply before writing a word):**

| Filter | Why It Matters |
|--------|---------------|
| Company Size | 10-person startup ≠ 500-person enterprise |
| Role | Founder pain ≠ VP pain ≠ IC pain |
| Industry | Different language, different proof points |
| Trigger | Recent funding vs. hiring vs. launching |

**ICP Decision Tree by ACV:**

- **ACV < $10K:** Focus on ROLE (who has the pain?). Higher volume, faster cycles.
- **ACV $10K–$50K:** Focus on COMPANY SIZE + ROLE. Balance personalization and volume.
- **ACV > $50K:** Focus on INDUSTRY + SPECIFIC PAIN. Lower volume, deeper research.

**Messaging by Segment:**

| Segment | Lead With | Avoid |
|---------|---------|-------|
| Founders (10–50 people) | Speed, doing more with less | Enterprise case studies |
| VP/Director | Looking good to leadership | "I know you're busy" |
| Enterprise | Risk reduction, proven at scale | Move-fast language |
| Agencies | Client results, efficiency | B2B SaaS examples |

**4 Questions to Answer Before Writing Any Segment:**
1. What's the #1 pain right now?
2. What do they actually care about? (Speed? Scale? Cost? Compliance?)
3. What language do they use?
4. What proof would resonate?

## Chapter 8: Follow-Ups — The 5-Email Sequence

**Key data point: 65% of replies come from follow-up emails 2–5.**

| Email | Angle | Template |
|-------|-------|---------|
| 2 | **Insight Add** | "Just ran the numbers on a similar campaign. The single biggest lever was [insight]. Lifted replies from 3% to 11%. Happy to share the breakdown." |
| 3 | **Social Proof** | "[similar company] was dealing with the same thing. Went from [before] to [after] in [timeframe]. Think this could work for {{company}}?" |
| 4 | **Free Resource (no ask)** | "Figured I'd send this regardless of timing. Here's [resource] — no call required. If you use it and want to chat, I'm around." |
| 5 | **Permission Close** | "{{firstName}}, totally fine if now's not the right time. Should I close this out, or worth revisiting in a few months?" |

**What NOT to write in follow-ups:**
- "Just bumping this up"
- "Circling back on my last email"
- "Haven't heard from you"
- "I'm sure you're busy but…"

**Special Angle Templates:**
- **Humor:** "{{firstName}}, are you one of those [role] who already has everything dialed, or just really good at ignoring emails? Because honestly, both are impressive."
- **Algorithm:** "{{firstName}}, I'm not giving up on you — but Gmail will break up with ME if I send another reply-less email. Can't afford couples therapy with an algorithm."
- **Direct Close:** "Looks like timing isn't right — totally get it. I'll close this out for now. If [specific outcome] becomes a priority, you know where to find me."

## Chapter 9: The 7 Golden Rules

| Rule | Why It Matters |
|------|---------------|
| Bad personalization > no personalization | Fake personal is worse than honest generic |
| Follow-ups > first email | 65% of replies come from touches 2–5 |
| One CTA only | Multiple options = no action |
| Under 80 words | Shorter emails get more replies |
| Lowercase subject lines | Feels human, not a campaign |
| No links in first email | Links trigger spam filters |
| Ask before you pitch | Earn the right to talk about yourself |
