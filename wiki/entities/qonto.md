# Qonto

*French neobank for SMBs/freelancers; [[entities/maxime-champoux|Maxime Champoux]]'s employer for ~7 years, where he rose to Head of Products. Scaled from ~50 to ~1,600 employees and (per Champoux) ~25,000 to ~600,000 SMB customers during his tenure [figures as stated by the practitioner, not independently verified].*

## Background

Initially ran its banking operations on a third-party banking-as-a-service (BaaS) provider (heard in the transcript as "Trésor" — likely Treezor [medium confidence]) before internalizing its own Core Banking system, a project Champoux led in 6 months by applying expertise built over 2.5 years doing the same thing at [[entities/ibanfirst|iBanFirst]]. Following a raise Champoux describes as ~$500M from Tiger Global (~4 years before this recording; unverified against public record in this source), the explicit strategic priority became shipping velocity as competitive defensibility — recruiting 36 product managers in 12 months and scaling the product org to ~150 people at a ~1:8 PM-to-engineer ratio.

## Role in This Wiki

Source of the wiki's Toyota-Production-System-inspired software engineering culture ("the Qontoway") — see [[concepts/toyota-production-system-for-software]] — and the pre-AI baseline against which Champoux measures the velocity transformation he later achieved at [[entities/well|Well]] with a team two orders of magnitude smaller.

## Key Contributions / Actions

- Internalized Core Banking from a third-party BaaS provider to capture interchange-revenue margin and control its own roadmap/quality bar, rather than sharing a provider's development capacity across multiple clients.
- Ran "the Qontoway": software-as-assembly-line, zero-bugs-in-production standard, Andon-cord-style stop-the-line practice, root-cause-driven continuous improvement.
- Scaled its product organization from 2 people to 150 (roughly 50 PMs plus designers, product marketers, UX writers) to keep pace with growing product-surface complexity, forcing specialization into squads.
