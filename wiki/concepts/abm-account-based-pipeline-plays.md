# ABM Account-Based Pipeline Plays

*Synchronize SDR outbound, marketing-run display ads, and physical direct mail on the same tight account list and the same message — reported breakthrough rates of ~10% on an "okay" campaign and ~20% on a great one against a 20-25 account list.*

## Overview

[[entities/armand-farrokh|Armand Farrokh]]'s account-based marketing (ABM) approach for enterprise, six-figure-deal prospecting into venture capital funds at [[entities/carta|Carta]] — a use case that sits between pure cold outreach and inbound demo requests, aimed at accounts too high-value and too low-volume to run through a standard sequence-based motion.

## Key Properties / Characteristics

- A tight, curated account list (Carta's example: ~25 accounts) rather than a broad list run at sequence volume.
- Multiple channels hit the same account simultaneously with synchronized messaging: SDR outreach, marketing-run display ads, and ABM-sent physical goods.
- A specific sector/angle framing per campaign (e.g., a competitor-takedown angle, or funds inside a particular compliance window) rather than generic outreach.
- Executive-to-executive escalation layered on top (CEO directly messaging target-account leaders).

## How It Works

Pick a specific sector angle to organize the campaign around. While SDRs work the ~25-account list directly, marketing runs display ads carrying the same core messaging to the same accounts, and ABM sends physical goods tied to the message (Carta's example: literal pies mailed to VC funds, with copy reading "Carta helps you track your slice of the pie"). Over-the-top executive outreach — the CEO personally messaging fund leaders — adds a final layer. The intent is genuine "surround sound": a target account sees the same message across ads, receives a tangible physical touch, gets direct SDR outreach, and may also hear from the sending company's CEO — all inside a short window, on a small enough list that this level of coordinated effort per account is economically justified.

**The SDR leader's role in making this work**: Farrokh frames his own job in this process as a "train traffic conductor" for marketing alignment — marketing's standard MQL scoring often doesn't reflect what actually produces booked meetings, so the sales leader needs to reverse-engineer the last 20 actual booked meetings with marketing to build realistic scoring criteria, and personally drive account-list selection rather than accepting whatever list marketing hands over, using marketing specifically for multi-channel "air cover" support around that self-selected list.

## Contradictions & Open Questions

- "Breakthrough rate" is used without a precise operational definition in the source — treat the 10%/20% figures directionally rather than as a benchmarkable KPI.
- No cost data given for the physical-goods component, making it hard to assess ROI relative to a pure digital ABM play at the same account-list size.
- Open question: does this approach's economics hold at accounts lists larger than ~25, or is the physical-goods/CEO-outreach layer specifically only viable at this scale of list?
