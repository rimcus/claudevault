# SDR Hiring Signal (Why Prior Experience Is Overrated)

*Prior SDR experience is a weak predictor of SDR success — none of Armand Farrokh's top 5 SDR hires had done the job before. A 3-round interview funnel (screen for proactivity, a two-pass mock cold call testing coachability, an on-site "super round") screens for the traits that actually predict performance instead.*

## Overview

[[entities/armand-farrokh|Armand Farrokh]]'s hiring data point, drawn from roles at [[entities/carta|Carta]] and [[entities/pave|Pave]]: of his top 5 SDR hires ever, not one had prior SDR experience — and he believes the same holds across his top 10 best SDR hires/transitions. Named examples span two recurring profiles: career changers who'd already demonstrated an ability to learn, write, or communicate (an ex-Hollywood scriptwriter, a pre-law grad, a VC-firm intern, a BD professional from a media company), and fresh college grads who'd shown proactivity through extracurriculars (athletes, strong internships, debate). The interview process itself is redesigned around testing for these traits directly rather than screening resumes for keyword-matched experience.

## Key Properties / Characteristics

- Three sequential rounds: screen → mock cold call → on-site "super round."
- The mock-cold-call round is run twice specifically to isolate coachability as its own measurable signal, distinct from raw call skill.
- The on-site round combines three different lenses in one sitting: written communication (email), structured thinking (research/targeting), and cultural fit (peer interview).
- A hiring manager alone is treated as insufficient for the cultural-fit read — an existing, bought-in rep runs part of the process specifically because they have insider visibility a manager lacks.

## How It Works

**Round 1 — the screen (phone/Zoom).** Selects for proactivity and communication ability over resume pedigree. The clearest illustrative example: [[entities/armand-farrokh|Farrokh]]'s top-ever SDR hire, Alan Chen, got the role via an unsolicited cold-email Loom video that wasn't actually tailored to Farrokh specifically but was constructed well enough (tone, personal story, a direct callout) that it read as genuinely personal — Farrokh's insight was that Chen wasn't just pitching himself, he was demonstrating the exact "personalize at scale" skill the job requires.

**Round 2 — the two-pass mock cold call.** Candidates receive a basic framework in advance (at Pave: a specific 30MPC podcast episode plus the company website) so the test isn't "can you cold call cold" but "can you apply a taught framework under ambiguity." This tests two things: (1) whether the candidate transforms raw information into an actual talk track (using a taught opener, correctly identifying the problem being solved) versus just regurgitating the website or defaulting to small talk; (2) coachability — after call one, the candidate self-critiques first ("what went well, what didn't"), receives one or two specific pieces of feedback, then re-runs the call. Farrokh's predictive claim: candidates who show no change between the two calls are gone within three months on the job; candidates who show a drastic change go on to succeed. [[entities/jason-bay|Jason Bay]]'s team runs a parallel, formalized 1-3 coachability rubric: 1 = not coachable, doesn't ask for feedback; 2 = receptive but doesn't proactively ask; 3 = actively asks for feedback and demonstrably implements it.

**Round 3 — the on-site "super round."** Three components in one sitting: a mock cold email (reveals writing mechanics directly, plus a modern red flag — a candidate who can't write a passable email without leaning on AI/ChatGPT); a targeting/research brain-teaser with the hiring manager (tests structured thinking on an unfamiliar prompt); and a free-form peer interview run by an existing, culturally-bought-in rep with license to test grit and fit however they choose — one example given is an oddball scenario question ("sell 17 cups of hot cocoa on a hot day in Florida") used to see how a candidate reacts to being thrown off-script, plus an informal "beer test" for whether the team would want this person around after a hard day of selling.

## Contradictions & Open Questions

- The claim about prior-experience hires is drawn entirely from Farrokh's own hiring history at two companies — no comparison group of prior-experience hires who *did* succeed is presented, so the strength of the anti-correlation is unclear beyond "his personal top 5 happened to have none."
- Open question: does the two-pass coachability test's predictive power hold for more senior/experienced sales hires, or is it specifically calibrated for entry-level SDR hiring where the baseline skill gap is larger and thus easier to observe changing?
