# Event Pipeline Pre-Booking & Floor Hunting

*For major industry events, the pipeline value is captured almost entirely before the event, not by staffing a booth passively during it — pre-book meetings ahead of time, and use "floor hunting" to convert the attendee list itself into a targeted account list on-site.*

## Overview

[[entities/armand-farrokh|Armand Farrokh]]'s core claim: "the money is 100% made before the event." Documented against major industry conferences (his examples: HR Transform, Dreamforce), this reframes event ROI away from booth traffic and toward pre-event outreach discipline plus a specific on-site tactic for converting the event's own attendee list into a targeted account list.

## Key Properties / Characteristics

- Pre-booking is tracked as a weekly leading metric for three months before the event, not left to the final week.
- Reps cross-book meetings for each other, not only for their own accounts — treating the event as a shared team goal rather than an individual one.
- "Floor hunting" repurposes the event's own registration/badge infrastructure as a targeting data source.

## How It Works

**Pre-booking.** Reps work their entire existing book (and cross-book for each other) to lock in roughly 30 meetings ahead of a major event, rather than relying on organic booth conversations during it. Progress is tracked as a weekly "Road to [Event]" booked-meeting count for the three months leading up to the event, turning event prep into an ongoing, measured campaign rather than a last-minute scramble.

**Floor hunting.** On-site, the tactic is to befriend whoever is checking badges at the entrance, ask to photograph the attendee/guest list under the pretext of checking whether a friend is on it, run that photographed list through ChatGPT to filter for ICP-matching accounts, and then target those specific people directly on the show floor — converting a passive, badge-scanning presence into an active, list-driven targeting exercise using data the event itself already has, rather than data the sales team would otherwise have to build independently.

## Contradictions & Open Questions

- The "floor hunting" tactic depends on being able to get someone to share or show the attendee/guest list — no discussion of how reliably this actually works, how event staff typically respond, or any data-privacy/event-policy considerations.
- Open question: does the pre-booking discipline generalize to smaller or more niche events with less predictable/trackable attendee lists, or does it depend on the scale of events like Dreamforce/HR Transform specifically?
