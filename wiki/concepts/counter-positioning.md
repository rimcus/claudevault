# Counter-Positioning

*Taking a competitor's core positioning claim and building your brand around its literal opposite, turning their stated strength into your differentiator.*

## Overview

Defined directly by [[entities/luke-harries|Luke Harries]] in contrast to the other valid competitive posture he names — the YC-style approach of ignoring competitors entirely and building for customers. Counter-positioning is explicitly a *marketing* strategy, not a product-development one: it works when an incumbent's positioning has hardened into an assumed market norm, so that inverting it reads as an obvious upgrade rather than an arbitrary difference.

## Key Properties / Characteristics

- Requires an incumbent whose positioning is already well-known enough that its opposite is legible to the market without extra explanation.
- Works best as a *bundle* of opposites across several axes at once (format, values, aesthetic), not a single inverted claim.
- Distinct from generic "differentiation" — the opposite must be framed as correcting a flaw in the incumbent's position, not just describing a different feature set.

## How It Works

**Example 1 — Ramp vs. Brex.** Brex's positioning centered on card rewards/points ("look how many points and rewards we give"). Ramp took the literal opposite position: points are framed as a waste of money that gets spent on "stupid things," and Ramp instead redirects that value into free software, with messaging that reduces the number of "imaginary points" and increases real savings. The result, per Harries, is that Ramp successfully got the whole market thinking of points themselves as a liability — every time Brex reinforced its points positioning, Ramp had a ready counter-message.

**Example 2 — TBPN vs. All-In.** TBPN is described as counter-positioned against the All-In podcast on nearly every axis simultaneously: ads (TBPN embraces heavy ad presence vs. All-In's none), stance toward tech (TBPN explicitly pro-tech vs. traditional media's anti-tech drift), production style (raw/live streaming vs. polished), and even wardrobe (smart/formal dress vs. hoodie tech-bro style). Harries frames this as "genius marketing" despite TBPN's product/format still being early.

**Example 3 (unprompted, from the interviewer) — 20VC vs. long-form podcasting.** [[entities/harry-stebbings|Harry Stebbings]] independently identifies his own show's short/concise format as a counter-position against 3-hour-plus long-form podcasts (e.g., Joe Rogan-style). Notes the irony that 20VC is now itself the incumbent that newer, even-shorter shows are counter-positioning against.

## Contradictions & Open Questions

- Counter-positioning depends on the incumbent not adapting — no discussion in-source of what happens once the incumbent absorbs or neutralizes the counter-claim (e.g., if Brex changed its own points messaging).
- Open question: how do you counter-position in a category with no single dominant incumbent (the 10–15 competitor landscape Harries describes elsewhere in the same source for AI companies)?
