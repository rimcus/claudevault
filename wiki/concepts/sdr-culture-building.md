# SDR Culture Building (Dial Blitz + Public Recognition)

*A daily, synchronized team-wide cold-calling block plus real-time public recognition of booked meetings — designed to replace SDR isolation and AE/SDR antagonism with visible camaraderie and cross-team cheering.*

## Overview

[[entities/armand-farrokh|Armand Farrokh]] describes the loneliness of prospecting as a real cultural problem he experienced firsthand as an AE at [[entities/carta|Carta]] before ever leading an SDR team: arriving early, clearing his inbox, then cold-calling into total floor silence because no one else wanted to prospect. As an SDR leader, he engineered the opposite condition deliberately — a shared, visible, competitive ritual rather than an individually miserable task everyone tries to avoid or minimize.

## Key Properties / Characteristics

- A fixed daily time block (9:00-10:00 AM) where the entire SDR team dials simultaneously, including the leader.
- Publicly visible competition framing (SF vs. Salt Lake, AE vs. SDR) run in a company-wide Slack channel, not a private team channel.
- Real-time, specific recognition: every booked meeting posted immediately with company name, booking method, meeting date, and the responsible AE tagged by name.
- A leader-modeled confidence practice: cold-calling loudly and visibly on the open floor, treated as a deliberate technique, not incidental behavior.

## How It Works

**The dial blitz.** Every SDR picks up the phone at the same moment for a fixed hour, framed as a competition and tracked in a channel the whole company (not just sales) can see. The immediate posting of booked-meeting details — with the AE tagged — flips the typical dynamic where AEs treat SDR-sourced meetings skeptically into one where AEs are cheering SDR wins in public, breaking down the usual AE/SDR silo (see also §16 in the source's full guide on AE/SDR power dynamics). The social pressure runs both directions: it becomes visibly unusual to be the one person not dialing or not posting during the block.

**Modeling confidence through volume, not privacy.** Farrokh describes a specific mental technique for cold-calling in front of colleagues: recognizing a "negative confidence spiral" (insecurity lowers your voice → you get rejected → you look around self-consciously → the prospect senses the insecurity on the call) and deliberately countering it by "getting big" — accepting in advance that colleagues will hear you flop and using loud, repeated, public practice itself to build genuine confidence, rather than retreating to a private phone booth to avoid the discomfort of being overheard.

**Elevating SDR status company-wide.** Beyond the daily floor culture, Farrokh used a company-wide "Show and Tell" presentation (to ~400 employees) to reframe how the rest of the org perceived SDR work — opening with a *Pursuit of Happyness* movie-scene analogy (the one meeting that changes everything, after enduring relentless rejection) paired with hard before/after activity numbers, shifting perception from "SDRs are spamming people" to genuine respect for a grueling, repeatable-under-pressure job.

## Contradictions & Open Questions

- No discussion of whether the dial-blitz format works for fully remote/distributed SDR teams, where the "floor" dynamic (overhearing colleagues, physical presence) doesn't exist in the same way.
- Open question: does the public-competition framing (SF vs. Salt Lake, AE vs. SDR) risk unhealthy competitive dynamics at some team sizes or cultures, or does it reliably stay positive? The source only describes the positive outcome case.
