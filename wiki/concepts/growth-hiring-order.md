# Growth Hiring Order

*The sequence in which a company should make its first growth hires, and the reasoning for not skipping steps.*

## Overview

[[entities/luke-harries|Luke Harries]]' standard advice to founders building their first growth function, drawn from his own experience joining [[entities/elevenlabs|ElevenLabs]] as employee-adjacent hire #1 in growth (the team was three very junior specialists with no one who'd held a growth role before).

## Key Properties / Characteristics

The core principle behind the order: **hire breadth before depth.** A specialist hired too early (a product marketer alone, or a channels-only person alone) leaves a structural hole — great messaging with no audience, or reach with no resonance.

## How It Works

1. **One generalist growth marketer**, first. Responsible for everything from messaging and positioning (working directly with the founder) through to awareness, channel setup, and testing. The critical hiring filter: they must deeply understand the product itself — technical, if selling to developers; consumer/virality-minded, if selling to consumers. Explicitly do *not* split this into two separate hires (a product marketer and a channels person) — each alone fails without the other.
2. **A growth engineer, front-end leaning** — a "hacky" software engineer comfortable running toward metrics. Responsible for SEO landing pages, mini-tools (see [[concepts/seo-mini-tools]]), and automated outreach.
3. **A motion designer** — brought in-house once launch velocity demands it (see [[concepts/growth-video-strategy]]); can be tested cheaply first via a project-based freelance engagement before committing to a full-time hire.
4. **Additional channel specialists**, added only once a specific channel has already shown organic signs of life — not preemptively.

## Variants / Subtypes

Backend-focused growth engineers are named as another valuable later hire, alongside additional motion designers, once the team scales past the first few roles.

## Contradictions & Open Questions

- No guidance on team size/stage thresholds for triggering hire #2, #3, #4 — "signs of life" is qualitative, not quantified.
- Open question: does this hiring order assume the founder is already doing hands-on growth work before hire #1, or does hire #1 replace founder-led growth entirely? The source implies the former (hire #1 "works with the founder" on messaging) but doesn't say explicitly when founder-led growth should stop.
