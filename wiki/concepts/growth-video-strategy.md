# Growth Video Strategy

*Three video formats for growth launches, governed by one rule: nearly all viewer attention lives in the first 30 seconds.*

## Overview

[[entities/luke-harries|Luke Harries]] treats video as the "keystone marketing piece" of every [[concepts/launch-playbook|Tier 1 launch]] at [[entities/elevenlabs|ElevenLabs]], and argues video remains underrated as a growth medium precisely because platforms still algorithmically favor it and audiences still prefer a crisp video over reading a blog post. The strategic content is in *which format* to use for a given product/audience, and in the operating discipline of building for the first 30 seconds rather than the full runtime.

## Key Properties / Characteristics

- Three formats, each suited to a different product/audience combination.
- One universal rule: assume most drop-off happens after 30 seconds, so front-load the value proposition regardless of total video length.
- In-house production is preferred over contractors as soon as launch velocity requires fast turnaround (roughly one week from product-ready to launch).
- Cheap testing path exists before committing to a full-time hire.

## Variants / Subtypes

### 1. Motion design (animated graphics)
The default/safe choice for major (Tier 1) launches. Abstract by nature, which lets the team foreground core value props, mix in abstract UI elements, and stay catchy without needing a charismatic on-camera presence or elite editing skill. Best for complex products that need to communicate a lot quickly.

### 2. Founder-led (talking head)
Explicitly flagged as risky: it can become "ego-full," and unless the editing is Mr. Beast-level, a multi-minute monologue loses viewers. Named example: Superhuman's ~5-minute founder-led launch video, judged too slow — "by the time he's walked down the staircase, you've moved on." Better suited to more boring/lifestyle products (CPG, lifestyle) or when there's already a prominent creator behind the brand.

### 3. Screen-share
Fast to produce, works well specifically for a technical audience who wants to see and "nerd out" on the actual product and craft. Recommended tool: Screen Studio.

## How It Works

- **The 30-second rule**: put nearly all editorial attention into the first 30 seconds — quick intro, then straight to core value props. It's fine to extend the video to 5 minutes for viewers who stay, but budget the *effort*, not just the runtime, around the opening.
- **In-house vs. contractor**: the main historical failure mode at ElevenLabs was working with under-resourced motion-design contractors during the launch crunch window — recommendation is to bring motion design in-house as soon as the company can, because launch timelines (about a week from "product ready" to "launch") don't tolerate contractor handoff latency.
- **De-risked testing before hiring**: most motion designers will do a single project for $5,000–$10,000, which is affordable for any funded company and is explicitly framed as worth it — a single organic launch (200K–700K views) would cost far more bought as paid-media CPMs.
- **Where video's value might erode**: rising supply of video content is a real risk to discovery (raised independently by [[entities/harry-stebbings|Harry Stebbings]]), but Harries doesn't think the medium has lost effectiveness yet. The next shift he anticipates is toward live-streaming (citing the visibility boost X/Twitter gives live streams) — plausible for post-launch follow-up content, less clearly applicable to the launch moment itself.

## Contradictions & Open Questions

- No guidance given on video length/format for Tier 2 or Tier 3 launches — the detail here is Tier-1-specific.
- Open question: does the "first 30 seconds" rule hold identically across all three formats, or is it stated with motion design specifically in mind (where it's easiest to engineer a fast hook)?
